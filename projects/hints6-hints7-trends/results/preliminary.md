# Preliminary Results

## Analytic sample

- HINTS 6 (2022): n = 4,912
- HINTS 7 (2024): n = 5,926
- Pooled: n = 10,838

These are respondents with valid values for both `SocMed_TrueFalse` and `SocMed_MakeDecisions`.

## Weighted prevalence of using social-media information for health decisions

| Survey cycle | Difficulty judging truthfulness | Weighted prevalence | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| HINTS 6 (2022) | No | 13.4% | 9.8%–17.0% |
| HINTS 6 (2022) | Yes | 18.8% | 15.8%–21.7% |
| HINTS 7 (2024) | No | 15.1% | 12.5%–17.8% |
| HINTS 7 (2024) | Yes | 24.2% | 21.8%–26.6% |

## Primary interaction model

The primary question is whether the association between difficulty judging truthfulness and using social-media information for health decisions differs between 2022 and 2024.

- interaction OR = **1.20**
- 95% CI = **0.83–1.73**
- two-sided p = **0.330** (t distribution, df=98)

### Interpretation

The point estimate is compatible with a stronger association in 2024, but the confidence interval includes 1 and the interaction test is not statistically significant. The analysis therefore **does not provide clear evidence that the exposure–outcome association changed between HINTS 6 and HINTS 7**.

This distinction matters: the descriptive prevalence gap is larger in 2024, but that visual difference should not be described as evidence of a statistically established change in association.

## Additive pooled model

Without the interaction term:

- difficulty judging truthfulness: OR **1.65**, 95% CI **1.38–1.98**
- HINTS 7 vs HINTS 6: OR **1.32**, 95% CI **1.05–1.66**

This additive model is descriptive/associational and adjusts only for the focal exposure and survey cycle. It is not a causal trend model.

## Design caveat

This is repeated cross-sectional analysis of independent survey samples, not longitudinal follow-up of the same individuals.
