# Single-Coder Pilot Notes

## Status

This is a **single-coder feasibility pilot**, not an inter-rater reliability result.

No kappa statistic should be reported until an independent second coder completes the same artifacts without seeing coder 1's scores.

## PUB-001 — breathing article

Public source:
https://starrchaige.github.io/wei-he-ni-yue-shen-hu-xi-shen-ti-yue-jin-zhang/

Preliminary coding rationale:

- purpose is clear;
- language is highly accessible and uses analogies to explain physiology;
- references are listed and several DOI-level sources are supplied;
- the article contains explicit self-assessment and stepwise practices;
- it therefore scores high on actionability and behaviour-change support;
- however, several physiological statements are framed with very high certainty;
- some self-test thresholds are presented as if they map directly to severity/state without visible validation support;
- higher-risk practices (breath holds, mouth taping) receive some contraindication language, but the boundary is not as specific as the advice itself.

Codebook implication:
`uncertainty_calibration` and `safety_boundary` appear able to capture dimensions that are not represented by evidence citations or actionability alone.

## PUB-002 — Coursepack public repository communication draft

Public source:
https://github.com/starrchaige/sisfit-coursepack/blob/main/xiaohongshu_post.md

Preliminary coding rationale:

- purpose and audience are explicit;
- the communication gives clear ways to use the tool;
- the artifact repeatedly limits diagnostic/treatment scope and names red-flag situations;
- causal/diagnostic claims are intentionally avoided;
- scientific evidence traceability is coded NA because the artifact is primarily product/scope communication rather than a substantive health-science explanation.

Codebook implication:
the framework needs `NA` handling so communication-purpose artifacts are not penalised for lacking academic citations when no substantive evidence claim is being made.

## Pilot conclusion

The coding system is feasible enough to move to a two-coder pilot, but two issues need explicit monitoring:

1. whether coders can distinguish **uncertainty calibration** from **causal-claim strength**;
2. whether coders apply **safety boundary** consistently across low-risk and high-risk advice.

No substantive claim about SISFIT's overall content quality should be drawn from these two items.
