Independent Secondary Analysis

HINTS 7: Social-Media Health Information Judgement & Use

Reproducible · Cross-sectional · 2026

When judging health information is difficult, how is social-media information used?

Secondary analysis of the U.S. National Cancer Institute's nationally representative HINTS 7 (2024) public-use dataset. The project examines whether difficulty judging the truthfulness of social-media health information is associated with using social-media information for personal health decisions.

Primary result: adjusted OR 1.83 (95% CI 1.42–2.34; complete-case n=5,183), using the HINTS full-sample weight and 50 JK1 replicate weights.

Sensitivity: the association remained directionally consistent in a four-level proportional-odds model (OR 1.50, 95% CI 1.25–1.78) and across reduced/full adjustment specifications.

Interpretation: cross-sectional association only; no causal claim and no claim that a single survey item is a validated digital-health-literacy scale.

Repeated Cross-Sectional Secondary Analysis

HINTS 6 + HINTS 7: 2022–2024 Comparison

Reproducible · Repeated cross-sectional · 2026

Did the association between judging difficulty and health-decision use change between survey cycles?

Pooled analysis of HINTS 6 (2022) and HINTS 7 (2024), treating the cycles as independent repeated cross-sectional samples. The variance design follows the NCI HINTS merging-tool logic with 100 Rizzo replicate weights, jackknife multiplier 0.98 and 98 degrees of freedom.

Primary result: survey-year interaction OR 1.20 (95% CI 0.83–1.73; pooled n=10,838; p=0.330). The analysis does not provide clear evidence that the association changed between 2022 and 2024.

Descriptive context: weighted use of social-media information for health decisions was 18.8% in 2022 and 24.2% in 2024 among respondents reporting difficulty judging truthfulness; corresponding estimates were 13.4% and 15.1% among those not reporting difficulty.

Interpretation: repeated cross-sectional population comparison only; no within-person change or causal claim. The larger descriptive gap in 2024 is not presented as evidence of a statistically established change in association.

Independent Secondary Analysis

NHANES 2013–2014: Device-Measured Movement & Self-Reported Leisure Activity

Reproducible · Cross-sectional · Device data · 2026

How is self-reported leisure physical activity associated with device-measured movement?

Secondary analysis of CDC/NCHS NHANES 2013–2014 linking the day-level wrist physical-activity-monitor summary with GPAQ leisure-activity items, demographics and body measures. The device outcome is MIMS movement density, not MVPA minutes.

Primary result: higher self-reported leisure physical activity was positively associated with higher device-measured movement density (beta 0.00876, 95% CI 0.00347–0.01404; n=4,726; p=0.003) after adjustment for age, sex and BMI.

Robustness: the focal coefficient remained positive across five prespecified movement-data completeness rules (beta 0.00754–0.00876) and in models without BMI or with additional race/ethnicity and education terms.

Interpretation: cross-sectional association only. MIMS movement density and self-reported leisure activity are related but non-equivalent measures; neither is treated as a gold standard for the other. The primary 1,200-minute / 4-day completeness rule is analyst-defined.

Prospective Doctoral Direction

Working Questions Under Refinement

Learning Health in the Digital Age

Working theme: how digital health literacy, content design and platform environments may shape engagement with and comprehension of physical-activity guidance. The final doctoral question will be refined for the specific supervisory and project context.

  • How do different forms of health-content scaffolding affect comprehension and engagement?
  • Which digital engagement metrics, if any, are meaningful proxies for health-learning outcomes?
  • How do platform characteristics influence the translation of evidence-based physical-activity guidance?
  • How can digital health literacy be supported without sacrificing accessibility?
