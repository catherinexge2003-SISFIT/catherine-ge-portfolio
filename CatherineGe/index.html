From Practice to Research Questions

My background combines a Master of Teaching with years of health-education, curriculum and digital-content practice. That work made one limitation clear: platform metrics can describe attention, but they cannot establish whether people understood health information or changed behaviour.

That gap now drives my research direction. My current preparation combines systematic evidence synthesis, reproducible secondary-data analysis and formal research-design training, with qualitative and mixed-methods work as a developing doctoral direction.

Selected Research Project

HINTS 7 Secondary Analysis

A completed reproducible analysis demonstrating quantitative research practice beyond coursework.

Independent Secondary Analysis Reproducible · 2026

Social-Media Health Information Judgement & Use

Secondary analysis of the U.S. National Cancer Institute's nationally representative HINTS 7 (2024) public-use dataset, examining whether difficulty judging the truthfulness of social-media health information is associated with using social-media information for personal health decisions.

5,183

Primary complete-case sample

OR 1.83

Adjusted association · 95% CI 1.42–2.34

50 JK1

Replicate weights for variance estimation

Weighted prevalence: use of social-media information for health decisions

Difficulty judging truthfulness24.1%
No reported difficulty15.1%

Bars scaled to a 0–30% range for readability. Adjusted OR 1.83 (95% CI 1.42–2.34).

Methods: survey-weighted logistic regression, JK1 replicate-weight variance estimation, ordinal sensitivity analysis and model-specification checks.

Interpretation: cross-sectional association only; no causal claim and no claim that a single HINTS item is a validated digital-health-literacy scale.

Related Open Research Resource

Physical Activity Adherence — Digital Intervention Micro Map v0.1

身体活动依从性 — 数字干预微地图 v0.1

A small open evidence map aligned with Catherine's research interests in behaviour change, physical activity and digital health.

Joint Open Research · Catherine Ge + Starr Choi v0.1 Frozen · 10 Studies

How do digital interventions try to support sustained physical activity?

The map structures 10 selected adult digital physical-activity intervention studies by population, target behaviour, intervention components, behaviour-change mechanisms, duration, outcomes and time-dependent results.

Emerging design question: under what conditions does a digital self-monitoring + feedback loop remain behaviourally salient long enough to support physical-activity maintenance?

Attribution: this open research project is jointly maintained by Catherine Ge and Starr Choi. It remains methodologically distinct from Catherine's independent secondary-analysis projects while directly overlapping with her prospective doctoral interests.
