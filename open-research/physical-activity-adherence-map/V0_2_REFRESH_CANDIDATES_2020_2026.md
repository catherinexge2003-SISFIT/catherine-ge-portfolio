# v0.2 Candidate Refresh Pool — 2020–2026

Status: **candidate pool only**.  
This file does **not** add PA011+ records and does not modify frozen v0.1.

## Purpose

Refresh the evidence base around the v0.1 question without discarding the historical mechanism baseline.

The v0.2 direction is:

> After self-monitoring and feedback became near-default components of digital physical-activity interventions, what determines whether those mechanisms remain behaviorally salient long enough to support maintenance?

## Gate

Candidates must satisfy the existing v0.1 inclusion rules:

1. Adults (18+).
2. Physical activity/exercise is the target behavior.
3. Digital component is part of the intervention.
4. Observable behavioral outcome is reported.
5. Peer-reviewed paper plus DOI or equivalent stable record.

Additional v0.2 preference:
- post-2020 publication;
- intervention-component contrast is interpretable;
- maintenance/follow-up, adaptation, personalization, engagement, or mechanism attribution adds something not already represented in v0.1.

## Triage score

This is an editorial prioritization score, **not** an evidence-quality or risk-of-bias score.

Each dimension is 0–2:

- **Current relevance** — how well the design reflects current digital-intervention practice.
- **Mechanism novelty** — adds a mechanism/design question not already well represented in v0.1.
- **Maintenance relevance** — directly informs persistence, attenuation, or post-intervention maintenance.
- **Contrast clarity** — supports a reasonably interpretable component/comparator contrast.
- **Outcome quality** — behavioral outcome is objective or otherwise especially informative for the map.

Maximum = 10.

## Core refresh candidates

### R01 — Simoes et al. (2026)

**Paper:** Effects of Using a Smartphone App Combined With Behavior Change Techniques on the Level of Physical Activity Among Adults and Older Adults: Sequential Multiple Assignment Randomized Trial  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2196/73388  
**Design:** 24-week SMART; app + tailored messages; app + tailored messages + gamification; educational control; nonresponders rerandomized after 6 weeks.  
**Key mechanisms:** adaptive intervention, tailoring, goal setting, self-monitoring, ranking, virtual badges/gamification, response-based escalation.  
**Behavioral outcomes:** daily steps, sedentary time, MVPA.  
**Why it matters:** moves the map from fixed treatment packages toward **adaptive treatment strategies** and explicitly confronts declining engagement over time.  
**Triage:** 10/10  
- Current relevance 2
- Mechanism novelty 2
- Maintenance relevance 2
- Contrast clarity 2
- Outcome quality 2

**Provisional disposition:** **A — core candidate**

---

### R02 — Sagelv et al. / ONWARDS (2025)

**Paper:** Online interventions to increase physical activity levels in self-reported inactive adults: the ONWARDS randomised controlled trial  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2023-001816  
**Design:** 18-month hybrid type I effectiveness-implementation RCT; inactive adults randomized to:
- tracker + Personal Activity Intelligence app;
- the above + home-based online training;
- the above + online peer support.

**Key mechanisms:** self-monitoring, personalized activity metric/feedback, structured training, peer/social support.  
**Behavioral outcome:** physical activity over 18 months.  
**Why it matters:** unusually long follow-up and a clean test of whether **adding more digital components actually improves long-term activity**. The study directly addresses long-term effectiveness and attenuation.  
**Triage:** 10/10  
- Current relevance 2
- Mechanism novelty 2
- Maintenance relevance 2
- Contrast clarity 2
- Outcome quality 2

**Provisional disposition:** **A — core candidate**

---

### R03 — Vos et al. (2025)

**Paper:** Effectiveness of a Just-In-Time Adaptive App to Increase Daily Steps: An RCT  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amepre.2024.09.010  
**Design:** 176 adults; step-counter control vs step counter + dynamically tailored JITAI coaching.  
**Key mechanisms:** tailored feedback, behavior-change preference matching, contextual walking suggestions, just-in-time adaptation.  
**Behavioral outcome:** app-recorded daily steps.  
**Key result:** no significant average intervention effect on steps; **perceived usefulness significantly moderated the effect**.  
**Why it matters:** introduces a modern problem absent from early digital trials: **an intervention can be technically personalized yet work only when the user experiences it as useful**.  
**Triage:** 9/10  
- Current relevance 2
- Mechanism novelty 2
- Maintenance relevance 1
- Contrast clarity 2
- Outcome quality 2

**Provisional disposition:** **A — core candidate**

---

### R04 — Brickwood et al. (2021)

**Paper:** Effects of Activity Tracker Use With Health Professional Support or Telephone Counseling on Maintenance of Physical Activity and Health Outcomes in Older Adults: Randomized Controlled Trial  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2196/18686  
**Design:** 117 older adults; 12-month RCT comparing activity tracker + accredited exercise physiologist support, telephone counseling, and usual care.  
**Key mechanisms:** self-monitoring, tracker feedback, professional support versus nondigital counseling.  
**Behavioral outcome:** ActivPAL-measured daily steps at baseline, 3, 6, and 12 months.  
**Key result:** tracker and telephone-counseling groups maintained steps while usual care declined; between-group differences were not uniformly significant.  
**Why it matters:** a direct long-term maintenance comparison between **digital feedback/support and an established human-support alternative**.  
**Triage:** 8/10  
- Current relevance 1
- Mechanism novelty 1
- Maintenance relevance 2
- Contrast clarity 2
- Outcome quality 2

**Provisional disposition:** **A — core candidate**

---

### R05 — Yoshimura et al. (2022)

**Paper:** Long-term Effects of the Use of a Step Count–Specific Smartphone App on Physical Activity and Weight Loss: Randomized Controlled Clinical Trial  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2196/35628  
**Design:** 109 adults; 32-week smartphone step-count app RCT.  
**Key mechanisms:** step-count self-monitoring / app-based behavior support.  
**Behavioral outcomes:** steps and MVPA; body weight as a separate physiological/clinical outcome.  
**Key result:** significant group-by-time interactions for step count and MVPA, but no added weight-loss advantage.  
**Why it matters:** reinforces the v0.1 distinction **behavior change ≠ downstream physiological outcome** while providing a substantially longer app exposure than many early trials.  
**Triage:** 8/10  
- Current relevance 1
- Mechanism novelty 1
- Maintenance relevance 2
- Contrast clarity 2
- Outcome quality 2

**Provisional disposition:** **A — core candidate**

## Secondary candidates

### R06 — Hardcastle et al. (2021)

**Paper:** The wearable activity technology and action-planning trial in cancer survivors: Physical activity maintenance post-intervention  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2021.04.004  
**Design:** RCT; cancer survivors; 12-week intervention plus 12-week post-intervention follow-up.  
**Key mechanisms:** wearable activity technology + action planning.  
**Behavioral outcome:** accelerometer-measured MVPA.  
**Key result:** intervention-group MVPA remained elevated at 24 weeks; the increase was maintained after the intervention ended.  
**Why it matters:** unusually useful for **true post-intervention maintenance**, where active intervention exposure has ended.  
**Triage:** 7/10  
- Current relevance 1
- Mechanism novelty 1
- Maintenance relevance 2
- Contrast clarity 1
- Outcome quality 2

**Provisional disposition:** **B — strong maintenance candidate; narrower clinical population**

---

### R07 — Black & Brunet (2021)

**Paper:** A Wearable Activity Tracker Intervention With and Without Weekly Behavioral Support Emails to Promote Physical Activity Among Women Who Are Overweight or Obese: Randomized Controlled Trial  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2196/28128  
**Design:** 3 arms: tracker + weekly behavioral emails, tracker only, PA-guideline control; follow-up to 21 weeks.  
**Key mechanisms:** self-monitoring/tracker feedback plus optional weekly behavioral support email.  
**Behavioral outcomes:** walking and MVPA.  
**Key result:** within-group gains appeared in the email + tracker arm, but **no significant between-group differences for any outcome**.  
**Why it matters:** a modern analogue of the v0.1 “extra contact is not necessarily extra value” pattern.  
**Triage:** 7/10  
- Current relevance 1
- Mechanism novelty 1
- Maintenance relevance 1
- Contrast clarity 2
- Outcome quality 2

**Provisional disposition:** **B — useful component-contrast candidate**

---

### R08 — Chukwuemeka et al. (2025)

**Paper:** Mobile-application-based performance feedback improves physical activity behaviour compared to education alone: a randomised controlled trial  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s44247-025-00206-z  
**Design:** 150 university staff; mobile-app performance feedback + hourly reminders vs performance feedback only vs education control; 12 weeks.  
**Key mechanisms:** performance feedback, self-monitoring, reminder frequency.  
**Behavioral outcomes:** pedometer steps, Google Fit metrics, IPAQ PA, sedentary time.  
**Key result:** performance-feedback groups improved; hourly reminders showed additional benefit for steps and sedentary time in reported between-group analyses.  
**Why it matters:** provides a recent test of **feedback intensity / prompt frequency**, potentially contrasting with older null SMS-add-on studies.  
**Triage:** 7/10  
- Current relevance 2
- Mechanism novelty 1
- Maintenance relevance 0
- Contrast clarity 2
- Outcome quality 2

**Provisional disposition:** **B — useful recent prompt/feedback candidate; weak maintenance contribution**

## What should happen to the old v0.1 studies?

Do **not** delete them merely because they are old.

The older studies supply the historical baseline needed to answer a stronger question:

> What changed in digital PA intervention design over ~20 years, and which problems did not change?

A likely v0.2 architecture is therefore not “replace the oldest 5 with the newest 5.” It is:

1. **Foundation / early digital feedback** — selected early studies from v0.1.
2. **Tracker + self-monitoring era** — wearable/app studies.
3. **Personalization / contextual tailoring**.
4. **Adaptive / JITAI / SMART designs**.
5. **Long-term and post-intervention maintenance**.

This keeps historical contrast while preventing v0.2 from looking like a 2007–2019 snapshot.
