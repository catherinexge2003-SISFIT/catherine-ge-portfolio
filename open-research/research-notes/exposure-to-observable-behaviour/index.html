From Intervention Exposure to Observable Behaviour

An identification problem in real-world digital misinformation intervention research

A working methodological reading of two platform field studies involving Jon Roozenbeek (University of Cambridge). Pending Catherine's academic review: not yet approved for retention or external outreach.

Empirical anchors

Two real-platform studies

Study 1 — Twitter/X, 2025. Roozenbeek J, Lasser J, Marks M, et al. Misinformation interventions and online sharing behaviour: lessons learned from two pre-registered field studies. Royal Society Open Science. 2025;12(11):251377. DOI 10.1098/rsos.251377.
Study 2 — Instagram, 2026. van der Linden S, Louison-Lavoy D, Blazer N, Noble NS, Roozenbeek J. Prebunking misinformation techniques in social media feeds: Results from an Instagram field study. Harvard Kennedy School Misinformation Review. 2026;7(1). DOI 10.37016/mr-2020-193.
Empirical comparison

Delivery, judgement and behaviour are different endpoints

2025 · Twitter/X

Two preregistered longitudinal ad-campaign studies targeting a combined 967,640 accounts with an emotional-manipulation inoculation video or control. The investigators did not detect a meaningful improvement in posting/retweeting behaviour after intervention.

Exposure uncertainty

Platform audience matching prevented confirmation of which users actually saw the treatment. The article describes approximately 7.5% estimated exposure; this is not a verified individual-level receipt record. Analytic time windows also produced unstable inferences.

2026 · Instagram

A 19-second prebunking video was deployed through Story Feed ads targeting 375,597 users. An opt-in platform poll produced 806 responses. Correct recognition of emotional manipulation was 59.55% in the treatment group versus 38.21% in controls: about 21.4 percentage points higher.

Interpretive limits

The Instagram design was quasi-experimental, not fully individually randomized. Poll response was low (approximately 1%); users self-selected into viewing/responding. Follow-up effects five months later were group-level, with no confirmed repeated measurements on the same individuals.

The Instagram study also reported increased click-through to learn more about prebunking. This is an observed platform action, but not evidence that users subsequently checked specific misinformation or changed clinical or health behaviours.

Methodological distinction

What is measured at each transition?

campaign assignment / targeting ↓ message delivered / potentially seen ↓ individual receipt or exposure ↓ proximal judgement / recognition ↓ information seeking / sharing / real-world decision

A difference in recognition among poll respondents is not automatically an effect on the target population's downstream behaviour. Equally, a null result for posting among all targeted accounts cannot by itself establish that the intervention would have no effect if fully received.

Analyses conditioned on exposure or a voluntary poll response may select participants by motivation, prior beliefs or platform behaviour. Those analyses do not straightforwardly identify the same causal effect as randomized campaign assignment.

Interpretation awaiting review

Possible identification problem

The two studies expose a gap between intervention deployment, proximal information-appraisal outcomes and later observable behaviour. A subsequent study could define its estimand around assignment to a platform campaign, then explicitly measure or bound actual receipt and link it to subsequent actions. This would separate the primary intent-to-treat question from more assumption-dependent exposure-conditioned estimates.

Because the original authors already discuss non-compliance, sample selection and outcome limitations, this note is intended to sharpen the next design choice, not to present those limitations as an overlooked flaw.

Author-correctable methodological question

Which causal quantity remains identifiable?

In a future platform-based prebunking study, what is the minimum defensible combination of randomized campaign assignment, individually verified intervention receipt, and linked behavioural outcomes needed to distinguish limited implementation from a genuinely small behavioural effect? If platforms provide only aggregate impressions and selectively observed poll responses, should the primary causal estimand remain the effect of assignment on the target population, with exposure-conditioned analyses treated as exploratory? Could a prespecified sensitivity or partial-identification approach still support informative bounds when person-level exposure is unavailable?

The question is proposed for future methodological discussion; it does not imply author agreement.

Connection to Catherine's research scope

Prospective transfer to digital health information

A comparable measurement problem could arise when evaluating whether AI-mediated health information improves detection of unsupported claims and later changes verification or health decisions. Such a study would need to distinguish exposure to an AI answer, appraisal performance and consequential behaviour, with an appropriate health-specific population and design.

Evidence boundary. Neither empirical paper directly tests generative-AI health advice, physical-activity interventions or actual clinical decisions. Cross-platform differences cannot be attributed to differences in intervention efficacy because treatment format, populations, targeting, measures and designs also differ.
