When Does Supported Physical Activity Become Maintenance?

The acquisition-to-maintenance boundary when feedback, monitoring and community support continue after an initial programme.

A structural reading of Gavin et al. (2025), focused on a phase-definition problem: whether persistence under continuing support should be interpreted as maintenance, continued treatment, or a deliberately supported maintenance state.

Source study

The study explicitly targets the transition beyond initiation

Gavin JP, Holt LC, Muckelt PE, Sadler E, McDonough S, Barker M. Maintaining physical activity in people with long-term conditions following engagement in physical activity referral schemes: barriers, enablers, and intervention strategies. International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. 2025;22:102. DOI: 10.1186/s12966-025-01802-y.

The study examined how people with long-term conditions could maintain physical activity after physical-activity referral schemes (PARS). It combined stakeholder mapping, interviews and co-design workshops, with findings mapped to COM-B.

The authors operationalised maintenance as physical activity lasting at least three months after an intervention. They also distinguish the transition from behaviour initiation toward longer-term maintenance, highlighting autonomy and self-efficacy as important during that transition.

Interpretive boundary: the paper is a qualitative intervention-development study. It does not test a specific tapering strategy or experimentally compare supported versus unsupported maintenance.
What participants said they needed

Maintenance was associated with continued scaffolding, not simple withdrawal

Participants described professional feedback and monitoring, peer support and accountability as important for staying active. Professionals also emphasised group activity, enjoyment and social support. The co-design workshops prioritised an integrated pathway connecting healthcare, leisure and community sectors after the initial scheme.

The resulting model is therefore not “teach the behaviour, stop support, observe whether it survives.” It is closer to “build autonomy while preserving enough environmental and interpersonal support for behaviour to remain feasible.”

The phase problem

Elapsed time and withdrawal of support are not the same thing

A study can call an outcome a maintenance outcome because it is measured months after the initial programme. But if important intervention functions remain active — feedback, monitoring, professional contact, peer accountability or structured community access — the behavioural system has not necessarily entered an unsupported state.

acquisition phase → behaviour initiated under structured programme transition → autonomy / self-efficacy develop → support may change in intensity or provider maintenance phase? → behaviour persists → but some scaffolding may remain

This creates two separable questions. First, does the behaviour persist over time? Second, how much external support is still required for that persistence? A time-based maintenance definition answers the first but not necessarily the second.

Why this matters

For long-term conditions, “independence” may not require zero support

The paper explicitly argues that ageing, symptoms and multimorbidity can continue to alter capability and motivation. In that context, requiring complete withdrawal of support as the criterion for maintenance may be clinically unrealistic.

At the same time, if continuing professional or community input is substantial, calling the observed behaviour “maintenance” without describing the support state can hide an important implementation requirement.

Behavioural persistenceThe person remains physically active over time.
Support dependenceThe amount and type of external scaffolding still needed to preserve that behaviour.

These dimensions can vary independently. A person can show durable behaviour while still benefiting from low-intensity monitoring or social infrastructure.

Possible design implication

Maintenance trials could make the support transition explicit

One way to make the acquisition-to-maintenance boundary more interpretable would be to specify the support trajectory prospectively rather than define maintenance only by elapsed follow-up time.

high-support acquisition → planned taper / transfer → lower-intensity community or digital support → stable support state → long-term behavioural observation

Researchers could then report physical activity alongside the support state: what was still being delivered, how often it was used, and whether behaviour remained stable as structured support reduced or shifted to lower-intensity community resources.

This would not require assuming that “true” maintenance means zero support. It would instead make the dependence of maintained behaviour on continuing scaffolding visible.

Open methodological question

What marks the transition into maintenance?

When ongoing feedback, monitoring, peer accountability or professional/community support continues after the initial referral scheme, what criterion should distinguish maintenance from continued treatment? Would maintenance be better operationalised around a planned reduction or transfer of support, or can stable physical activity under continuing low-intensity scaffolding still count as maintenance once the behavioural locus has shifted toward autonomy?

A related design question is whether maintenance studies should report the support trajectory as part of the outcome context, rather than relying primarily on a threshold such as “three months post-intervention.”

Method boundary

What this note is

This is a research-development note for structural interpretation and external correction. It is not a re-analysis of the MOTH programme, a claim that ongoing support invalidates maintenance outcomes, or an argument that people with long-term conditions should become fully independent of healthcare or community support.

The original article remains authoritative for its methods and findings. Corrections from the study authors or relevant behaviour-change researchers should be recorded transparently and used to revise this working interpretation.
