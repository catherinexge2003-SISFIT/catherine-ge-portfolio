When a Digital Intervention Changes Intentions but Not Physical Activity

What counts as incremental value when exposure is incomplete and the comparator already contains self-monitoring and a step goal?

A structural reading of Remskar et al.'s digital mindfulness trial, focused on intervention contrast, exposure, proximal cognition and observed behaviour rather than a binary “worked / did not work” judgment.

Digital health Physical activity Behaviour change Active comparator Exposure / fidelity
Source trial

The design contrast is narrower than “digital intervention vs control”

Remskar M, Ainsworth B, Maynard OM, et al. Getting active through mindfulness: Randomised controlled trial of a digital mindfulness-based intervention promoting physical activity engagement and enjoyment. Mental Health and Physical Activity. 2025;28:100680. DOI: 10.1016/j.mhpa.2025.100680.

The preregistered 30-day trial randomised 109 insufficiently active university students across three English sites. Both groups received an activity monitor and an explicit goal of 8,000 steps per day. The intervention group received the same behavioural backbone plus a self-delivered digital mindfulness programme designed to support physical-activity engagement.

Active comparatorActivity monitor + 8,000-step/day goal
Incremental component30-day digital mindfulness intervention added to the same monitor + goal

This matters because the trial does not test whether tracking and goal-setting can support activity. Both groups already receive those components. The cleaner question is whether the mindfulness layer adds measurable value beyond an active self-monitoring / goal-setting comparator.

Observed outcomes

Something changed, but the behavioural contrast did not

Self-reported physical activity increased substantially in both groups and sedentary time decreased. However, the between-group condition-by-time effects for physical activity and sedentary time were not statistically significant. The added mindfulness layer therefore did not show a clear incremental behavioural advantage during the 30-day trial.

At the same time, the groups were not identical on all proximal outcomes. The mindfulness group showed a stronger increase in behavioural intentions to be active. Daily ecological momentary assessment also showed a significantly greater improvement in physical-activity confidence in the mindfulness group, while the pre-post exercise self-efficacy scale itself did not improve.

Exposure

Incomplete intervention use constrains the interpretation

Participants in the mindfulness condition were prescribed 30 sessions. Actual exposure was substantially lower: the median was 9.5 sessions. Fourteen intervention participants (27%) completed no full mindfulness session; 23 (44%) completed at least half of the prescribed sessions; and 8 (15%) completed all 30.

That makes a simple causal reading difficult. A null behavioural contrast cannot automatically be taken as evidence that the hypothesised psychological pathway failed, because a substantial fraction of participants did not receive the intended exposure.

However, exposure is not a complete explanation either. The authors conducted an exploratory sensitivity analysis excluding the 14 participants who completed zero sessions. Those pre-post comparisons were reported as largely consistent with the intention-to-treat analyses.

Working boundary: incomplete exposure weakens strong claims about mechanisms, but it does not by itself establish that better adherence would have produced an incremental behavioural effect.
Structural reading

The active comparator changes what a null result means

Because both groups already had an activity monitor and a concrete daily step target, the relevant contrast is incremental. The trial can therefore be represented more precisely as:

monitor + explicit step goal vs monitor + explicit step goal + digital mindfulness → exposure to added component: partial → proximal cognition: selective positive signals → incremental PA behaviour: not demonstrated → longer-term maintenance: not tested

This separates four questions that are often collapsed: Was the component assigned? Was it meaningfully received? Did proximal outcomes move? Did those changes translate into incremental behaviour?

What can be inferred

A narrow interpretation avoids two opposite errors

Too strong“The mindfulness mechanism failed.” The trial did not directly establish failure of a psychological mechanism, and exposure was incomplete.
Also too strong“The intervention would have worked with better adherence.” The engaged-participant sensitivity analysis does not provide evidence for that counterfactual claim.

A more defensible description is:

No incremental behavioural effect over an active wearable/goal comparator during the 30-day intervention, alongside incomplete intervention exposure and selective changes in proximal physical-activity cognitions.

This wording deliberately stays close to what was measured. It does not equate a proximal cognitive signal with meaningful behaviour change, but it also does not erase that signal simply because the behavioural endpoint was null.

Connection to the PA adherence map

A worked example, not PA016

This note applies the interpretive boundary being developed in the Physical Activity Adherence — Digital Intervention Micro Map v0.2. It does not add this trial to the canonical PA001–PA015 manifest.

digital component assigned ✓ → component actually used: partial → proximal cognition changes: some signal → incremental behaviour change: not demonstrated → sustained / maintenance behaviour: ?

The purpose is to test whether the working distinction between implementation/exposure evidence and observed behavioural effect remains useful when applied to a recent digital intervention outside the 15-study map.

Open methodological question

What evidence would make the proximal change behaviourally meaningful?

When an intervention changes proximal physical-activity cognitions such as intention or confidence, but does not produce an incremental behavioural effect over an active self-monitoring/goal comparator—and exposure is incomplete—what evidence would be needed before treating those cognitive changes as behaviourally meaningful rather than simply proximal outcomes that did not translate into behaviour?

A short correction, alternative interpretation, or methodological pointer is useful. The goal is not to obtain endorsement of the note, but to expose the interpretation to correction.

Method boundary

What this note is

This is a research-development note for structural interpretation and external correction. It is not a systematic review, causal mediation analysis, replication, risk-of-bias assessment, or claim about the effectiveness of mindfulness interventions generally.

The original article remains authoritative for its methods and results. Any correction from the study authors or relevant method experts should be recorded transparently and used to revise this working interpretation.
