Changing the Behavioural Unit Instead of Adding More Digital Support

A methodological reading of Bissett et al.'s co-designed micropatterns intervention.

The intervention makes an unusually consequential design move before the digital layer is added: it shifts the target from planned exercise sessions toward very brief bouts of incidental moderate-to-vigorous activity embedded in everyday routines. This note asks how a later trial could identify the value of that target shift itself.

Source study

The intervention starts by redefining what counts as the target behaviour

Bissett M, Koemel NA, Ahmadi M, et al. Boosting Everyday Movement: Co-designing a digital micropatterns intervention with socioeconomically diverse UK and Australian communities. Journal of Physical Activity and Health. Accepted for publication, 2026. Preprint: 10.1101/2025.10.29.25339035.

The study used three co-design workshops in Glasgow and Sydney and was guided by COM-B / the Behaviour Change Wheel, the MRC framework for complex interventions, and 6SQuID. Participants explored barriers and facilitators to vigorous or moderate-to-vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity (VILPA / MV-ILPA), then helped prioritise modifiable factors and intervention components.

The core behavioural proposal is not simply “exercise with an app”. It is to make brief, intense everyday actions — brisk walking, stairs, carrying loads, energetic household or transport activity — the object of behaviour change.

Analytic term: this note uses behavioural unit as shorthand for the granularity and form of the target behaviour. It is not terminology used by the authors.
Intervention logic

Lowering the coordination cost of action may be part of the intervention

Conventional structured activityUsually requires a protected block of time, preparation, a place or route, and a decision to “exercise”.
Micropattern targetCan be embedded inside existing routines and accumulated through very short bouts with minimal equipment or planning.

The co-design data support this distinction. Participants highlighted adaptability, dual-purpose activities, minimal time commitment, routine integration and short manageable movement as facilitators. Barriers included lack of knowledge, concerns about ability and safety, low mood, sociocultural constraints and environmental constraints.

This suggests a mechanism that is upstream of digital engagement: the target action itself may require less coordination, fewer resources and less dedicated time than structured exercise. If so, changing the behavioural unit could reduce the amount of support required to make action feasible.

structured exercise target → dedicated time + preparation + opportunity → digital support attempts to help execution micropattern target → action embedded in an existing routine → digital support may only need to make the opportunity visible, safe and repeatable
Co-designed support package

The behavioural target and the support package were developed together

The final six-week programme did not rely on the target shift alone. It combined education, training, goal setting and VILPA/MV-ILPA tracking. The co-design process identified thirteen intervention components spanning seven Behaviour Change Wheel functions: education, persuasion, training, environmental restructuring, modelling, incentivisation and enablement.

The digital component therefore does not replace the behavioural redesign. It sits around it.

Delivery mechanisms

The planned comparison varies support intensity within the same micropattern target

Because participants valued social components but expressed mixed views about digital accessibility, the intervention was configured for three delivery mechanisms: app only; app plus wearable; and app plus wearable plus two in-person workshops. All three retained the same micropattern behavioural target.

same target: brief everyday micropatterns APP vs APP + wearable vs APP + wearable + community workshops → varies delivery/support → does not vary the behavioural target itself

This is a useful design for asking how much technology and social contact are needed once micropatterns are already the target. It is not designed to estimate whether micropatterns are easier to adopt than a conventional structured-activity target under otherwise matched support.

Linked feasibility study

The next-phase results sharpen the question, but do not answer it

Koemel NA, Bissett M, Ryde GC, et al. Multi-Centre Randomised Controlled Feasibility Testing of a Physical Activity Micropattern Intervention Among Socioeconomically Diverse Women. medRxiv. 2025. DOI: 10.1101/2025.10.28.25339013.

The linked six-week feasibility study randomised participants to app only, app plus wearable, or app plus wearable plus community support. Completion was high overall, and the community-supported arm had no withdrawals. The largest observed increase in MVPA was also in that arm, but the sample was small and the study was designed for feasibility rather than powered efficacy comparisons.

The pattern is therefore compatible with several explanations: the micropattern target may itself be accessible; community support may add value; wearable feedback may or may not help in isolation; or these elements may interact. The current design cannot separate the first explanation from the others because all arms use the same target.

Important boundary: the co-design paper does not establish that “intervention burden causes declining engagement”. That premise would overstate the evidence. The narrower, testable issue is whether changing the target behaviour reduces participation burden independently of the support package.
Methodological hinge

What contrast would identify the value of changing the behavioural unit?

If the scientific question is whether micropatterns lower the practical burden of becoming active, then the causal contrast has to hold support intensity as constant while changing the target behaviour.

Micropattern armBrief incidental MVPA target + matched education + matched goal setting + matched tracking + matched contact.
Structured-MVPA comparatorConventional planned MVPA target + the same education dose + goal-setting structure + tracking + contact intensity.

A factorial design could go further by varying both target type and support intensity, allowing estimation of whether micropatterns reduce the marginal need for digital or social support. But that may not be the relevant estimand if the intended public-health intervention is deliberately a package rather than a separable mechanism.

Open methodological question

Would a matched-support active comparator be necessary?

If a future definitive trial wanted to estimate the value of changing the behavioural target itself — from structured exercise to brief everyday micropatterns — what comparator would be needed to separate that effect from the effects of digital and social support? Would a matched-support active comparator promoting conventional structured MVPA be necessary, or is the more meaningful estimand the effectiveness of the co-designed package as a whole?

This question is deliberately framed so that the authors can correct the premise. If the intervention theory treats the behavioural target and support architecture as inseparable, then attempting to isolate a “micropattern effect” may be methodologically neat but scientifically less relevant.

Method boundary

What this note is

This is a research-development note for structural interpretation and external correction. It is not a trial re-analysis, causal mediation study, formal process evaluation, or claim that micropatterns are superior to structured exercise.

The original articles remain authoritative for their methods and results. Any correction from the study authors or relevant method experts should be recorded transparently and used to revise this working interpretation.
