From Stay-Active App Design to Observed Physical-Activity Enactment

Moving from BCW specification through feasibility to a defensible activity-effectiveness trial

A paper-level methodological reading of research by Moscho Michalopoulou (University of Oxford). Pending Catherine review. No author participation or endorsement is implied.

A — physical activity, digital BCT delivery, pregnancy-specific clinical context

Primary published evidence

Smith R, Michalopoulou M, Reid H, et al. Applying the behaviour change wheel to develop a smartphone application 'Stay-Active' to increase physical activity in women with gestational diabetes. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. 2022;22:253. DOI: 10.1186/s12884-022-04539-9.

Version of Record, 2022-03-26.

Smith R, Gould R, Kenworthy Y, et al. A feasibility study using motivational interviewing and a smartphone application to promote physical activity (+Stay-Active) for women with gestational diabetes. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. 2024;24:360. DOI: 10.1186/s12884-024-06508-w.

Version of Record, 2024-05-14; non-randomised feasibility study.
Study design

What the authors actually studied

The 2022 paper applied the Behaviour Change Wheel (BCW), COM-B, TDF, patient input and stakeholder workshops to develop a smartphone application supporting an existing motivational-interviewing (MI) pathway for women with gestational diabetes mellitus. Four intervention functions and ten behaviour change techniques were specified. The 2024 follow-up examined feasibility and acceptability of combined MI plus app support, not comparative efficacy.

Results

What their evidence supports

In the 2024 non-randomised study, 67 women enrolled; 56 (83%) received the intervention at the relevant visit; 53 (79%) completed follow-up; 55 (82% of all enrolled) set an in-app goal. The primary endpoints included recruitment, retention, engagement, acceptability and fidelity. Objective activity measurement completeness declined from baseline to later assessment. The study cannot isolate the app's incremental effect over motivational interviewing or the normal pregnancy trajectory.

Methodological reading

A distinction for follow-up research

A component’s theoretical presence in the BCW map, technical use (e.g., setting a goal), and behaviour enactment (e.g., actually walking) require different measurements. This distinction is consequential when an app is bundled with clinician support and outcomes change over time. Declining accelerometer-data completeness further complicates interpretations of maintained physical activity.

Author-correctable question

A specific question worth testing

In a subsequent randomised evaluation of MI plus Stay-Active, what minimal factorial or comparator structure would let investigators separate the incremental app contribution from the MI/clinical-support backbone, while measuring goal enactment and objective activity over time? How should missing accelerometer data and transitions from enrolment to actual app use enter the prespecified analysis rather than appear only as descriptive engagement statistics?

This question invites correction and alternative study designs; it does not assert that the authors overlooked the point.

Connection to Catherine

Why this study matters to her research scope

Catherine's Physical Activity Adherence Map and BCT enactment notes already distinguish implementation exposure from behavioural effects. The directly measured target here is physical activity; pregnancy and GDM are essential clinical constraints and should not be generalized to all healthy adults.

Limitations

What the study does not show

The 2022 article is a development study; the 2024 article is non-randomised feasibility. High goal-setting rates are not proof of causal effectiveness, and concurrent clinical support cannot be independently credited to the app.
