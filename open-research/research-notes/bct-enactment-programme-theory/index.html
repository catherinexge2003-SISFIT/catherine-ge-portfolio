From Intervention Theory to Demonstrated Mechanism

Is participant enactment of behaviour change techniques evidence of receipt, a mechanism of action, or both?

A structural reading of the Let's Move It programme-theory evaluation, focused on the layer between delivered intervention content, participants' use of behaviour change techniques, psychosocial determinants and physical activity.

Behaviour change theory Physical activity Programme theory BCT enactment Mechanisms of action Fidelity / receipt
Source study

The paper tests the programme theory rather than stopping at the outcome

Palsola M, Araújo-Soares V, Hardeman W, et al. Evaluating the Let's Move It intervention programme theory for adolescents' physical activity: Theorized psychosocial mechanisms of behavioural changes. British Journal of Health Psychology. 2025;30(1):e12744. DOI: 10.1111/bjhp.12744.

The analysis starts from an explicit distinction between action theory — intervention components changing theorized determinants — and conceptual theory — those determinants changing physical activity. The programme combined constructs from the reasoned action approach, self-determination theory and control theory.

This matters because a null behavioural effect can arise from at least two different failures: the intervention may not move its intended determinants, or the determinants may move without producing the expected change in behaviour.

Observed pattern

The intervention changed reported BCT use more clearly than its prespecified determinants

The intervention had few detectable effects on the theorized psychosocial determinants. The clearest immediate post-intervention difference was an increase in reported behaviour-change-technique use in the intervention arm. At the same time, most of the intended determinants did not show detectable intervention effects.

Parts of the conceptual theory received more support: several determinants were associated with physical activity, and autonomous motivation was associated with accelerometer-measured MVPA immediately after the intervention. Yet autonomous motivation itself was not detectably increased by the intervention on average.

intervention content delivered → participants report more BCT use → most prespecified determinants: no detectable intervention effect → MVPA: no demonstrated intervention effect but: autonomous motivation ↔ MVPA change without a demonstrated intervention → autonomous motivation effect
The missing layer

“BCT delivered” and “determinant changed” may not be adjacent causal steps

Intervention taxonomies usually describe what an intervention delivers. Programme theories then often map those techniques onto hypothesised mechanisms of action. But participants still have to notice, understand, adopt and enact what was delivered.

DeliveryA facilitator or intervention provides a BCT as intended.
Receipt / enactmentThe participant recognises or uses that technique in their own behaviour regulation.
Determinant changeA theorized construct such as autonomous motivation, self-efficacy or intention changes.
Behaviour changePhysical activity changes in the predicted direction.

The Let's Move It results make the middle layer visible. Participants reported greater use of the taught techniques, yet that signal did not translate into detectable average change in most prespecified determinants.

Two interpretations

The same BCT-enactment result can support different causal readings

InterpretationWhat BCT enactment meansWhere the programme theory may have failed
Receipt / fidelity interpretationEvidence that participants received and used intervention contentThe action theory failed after receipt: enacted techniques did not move the intended determinants
Mechanism interpretationAn intermediate behaviour through which delivered BCTs operateThe model may be missing a causal layer between intervention delivery and determinant change

These interpretations are not mutually exclusive. BCT enactment could be both an indicator of receipt and a behavioural mechanism. But treating it as both without specifying its temporal and causal position would make mechanism claims difficult to test.

Measurement problem

Temporal ordering becomes decisive

If BCT enactment, psychosocial determinants and physical activity are measured at the same broad follow-up points, it is difficult to distinguish whether technique use preceded determinant change, followed it, or simply co-occurred with it.

delivered BCT → noticed / understood → self-enacted BCT → determinant changes → behaviour changes requires evidence about: timing + within-person change + exposure / enactment + behavioural response

The paper itself highlights several additional sources of uncertainty, including possible ceiling effects and insensitive determinant measures. Those issues make a null determinant effect insufficient, on its own, to falsify the action theory.

What would strengthen mechanism evidence?

Demonstrating a mechanism requires more than naming the theory in advance

A stronger prospective test could separate intervention delivery, participant enactment and determinant change in time. Repeated or intensive longitudinal measurement could then test whether an individual's increase in use of a particular BCT precedes a predicted change in the corresponding determinant and subsequently predicts behaviour.

This would also distinguish a population-average null from heterogeneous individual pathways. A technique could be actively enacted by some participants and useful for them even if the average determinant score remains unchanged.

Working boundary: a specified programme theory is a causal hypothesis. A delivered BCT, participant-reported BCT enactment, determinant change and behavioural change are distinct pieces of evidence and should not be collapsed into a single claim that a mechanism was “activated”.
Open methodological question

Where should BCT enactment sit in the programme theory?

When participants report greater enactment of BCTs taught by an intervention, but the prespecified psychosocial determinants do not detectably change, should BCT enactment be treated mainly as evidence of intervention receipt/fidelity — implying that the action theory then failed to move the intended determinants — or should self-enacted BCT use itself be modelled as an intermediate mechanism between delivered intervention content and changes in determinants or behaviour? If it is a mechanism, what temporal evidence would be sufficient to distinguish delivered BCT → enactment → determinant change → behaviour change?

The question is deliberately framed so that the authors can reject the proposed causal ordering. The relevant issue is not whether the intervention was “theory-based”, but where the empirical evidence allows each theorized link to be placed.

Method boundary

What this note is

This is a research-development note for structural interpretation and external correction. It is not a re-analysis of the Let's Move It data, a formal mediation analysis, or a claim that the programme theory was falsified.

The original article remains authoritative for its methods and findings. Corrections from the study authors or relevant behaviour-change researchers should be recorded transparently and used to revise this working interpretation.
