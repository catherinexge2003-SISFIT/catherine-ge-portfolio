From Evidence Learning to Evidence-Informed Decisions

What should an intervention measure before it can claim that evidence changed public-health practice?

A paper-level working interpretation of research by David Nunan (University of Oxford). Pending Catherine review; no author contact or endorsement implied.

Primary literature and versions

Original evidence

Groot E, Pelley J, Boylan A-M, Nunan D. Interventions to Enable or Improve Evidence-Informed Decision Making in Public Health and Preventive Medicine: A Scoping Review. AJPM Focus. 2026;5(3):100473 (first online December 2025). DOI: 10.1016/j.focus.2025.100473.

Published journal version; first online 2025-12-11, issue June 2026.
Study design

What was actually studied

A scoping review searched published and grey literature for interventions targeting evidence-informed decisions in public health and preventive medicine, including records from 1992 through March 2025. The final set comprised 17 reports describing seven distinct interventions. The reports largely described education and capacity-building activities, often with self-reported participant outcomes.

Reported evidence

What the paper found

Most reports described perceived learning, positive reactions or self-reported behaviour change. Only one organizational strategic planning intervention described organizational and population-level impact; the review did not identify an intervention specifically targeting public-health-and-preventive-medicine physicians. These observations show an evidence gap; they do not estimate that such training is ineffective.

Methodological reading

Distinguishing availability, exposure and outcome

Training uptake and confidence in evidence appraisal do not demonstrate that evidence is appropriately used when an actual decision must be made. A future evaluation could separate (a) evidence appraisal performance, (b) formulation of a recommendation for a particular patient or population, (c) whether recommendations change service delivery and (d) measured patient or population outcomes. Different levels require different outcome definitions and comparison designs.

Question for author correction

A specific methodological question

If evidence-informed decision-making training produces high self-reported learning but little directly observed practice evidence, what feasible decision task and follow-up measurement should be prespecified to test whether participants can correctly integrate research evidence with patient context? Could physical-activity counselling serve as a traceable case without reducing public-health decisions to individual clinical advice?

This is a proposed question, not a claim that the original authors overlooked this limitation or have agreed to collaborate.

Evidence boundary

What cannot be inferred

Scoping reviews map available studies; heterogeneity and self-reported outcomes prevent a pooled causal estimate of training on patient health. Outcomes labelled “success” were not all externally observed decisions.
